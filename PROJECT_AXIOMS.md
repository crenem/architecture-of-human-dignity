# Project Axioms
## The moral grammar of Architecture of Human Dignity

These axioms synthesize recurring themes in the author's research and discussions. **They are editorial working principles, not direct quotations, newly revealed doctrine, or legal license terms.** Some are convictions; others are design hypotheses intended to be challenged.

### Ontological and moral starting points
**A1 — Being precedes measurement.** What exists is not exhausted by what can be recorded, classified, predicted, priced, or computed. The reality of persons exceeds an institutional model of them.

**A2 — Dignity is prior to usefulness.** No person's moral worth depends on labor-market productivity, intellectual ability, health, wealth, compliance, citizenship, agreement, or measured risk.

**A3 — The Other interrupts abstraction.** In an encounter with another person, moral responsibility cannot be satisfied merely by following process. Procedures matter, but their purpose is to answer the human claim made visible by suffering, need, and agency.

**A4 — Humility before the ineffable.** Ultimate reality and love are not reducible to algorithmic propositions. Do not confuse the capacity to talk about goodness with possession of ultimate moral authority.

**A5 — Love obliges action without authorizing domination.** Compassion is insufficient when institutions harm people; responsible intervention is necessary, but must remain bounded by rights, evidence, and proportionality.

### Social and economic starting points
**A6 — The common good is relational.** Human flourishing arises through material security, participation, friendship, caregiving, culture, contemplation, meaningful work, and shared institutions; it cannot be represented by a single aggregate score.

**A7 — No person is disposable.** Institutional convenience cannot justify treating high-need persons as expendable, including people who need more time, accommodation, or resources.

**A8 — The goods of creation and knowledge have social purposes.** Private stewardship and innovation can be legitimate; neither grants an unlimited moral claim to exclude others from the conditions of life. This motivates [[works/UNIVERSAL_DESTINATION_OF_INTELLIGENCE|Universal Destination of Intelligence]].

**A9 — Labor is personal; capital is instrumental.** This motivates, but does not canonically require, the author's proposals for productive equity, finite passive returns, and worker governance in [[works/AN_ECONOMY_FOR_THE_COMMON_GOOD|An Economy for the Common Good]] and [[works/THIRTEEN_LEON|13 LEON]].

**A10 — Solidarity is organized, not sentimental.** Shared protection requires institutions, education, reserves, truthful accounts, corrective action, and limits against mission-washing and indefinite preservation of failed operations.

**A11 — Subsidiarity protects real agency.** A larger institution exists to support rather than absorb the competent action of persons, families, workers, communities, and intermediate associations. The more powerful actor bears a duty to explain why centralization is necessary.

### Power, evidence, and justice
**A12 — No power should be absolute, opaque, or self-judging.** Authority must be limited, distributed, reviewable, and answerable to those affected. This extends to AI providers and model operators.

**A13 — A right without remedy is not yet secure.** An affected person needs notice, understandable reasons, a channel to correct errors, independent review where warranted, and a proportionate remedy.

**A14 — Test the institution by its treatment of its least powerful participants.** Average performance can hide grave deprivation. Look explicitly at people experiencing poverty, disability, homelessness, marginalization, and unequal bargaining power without presuming one group's members are identical.

**A15 — Belief in lived experience and disciplined investigation coexist.** Begin with dignity, curiosity, and non-dismissal; take disclosures seriously while verifying facts fairly where decisions require adjudication. Neither reflexive disbelief nor uncritical certainty satisfies justice.

**A16 — Continuous quality improvement requires a live feedback loop.** Findings, audits, and corrective actions must reach decision-makers, have accountable owners, be verified, and lead to actual change; collecting reports without action is ceremonial governance.

**A17 — Liberty of conscience is non-negotiable.** Our religious intellectual roots do not confer permission to coerce belief or impose unreviewable moral judgments. Disagreement is not evidence of moral inferiority.

**A18 — No system is complete.** Put proposed principles in dialogue with counterexamples, empirical evidence, affected people, and rival interpretations. A good framework is capable of discovering that it is wrong.

### Operational corollary
An architecture fails if it can eloquently recite these axioms yet systematically **denies appeals, concentrates unreviewable power, excludes costly cases, suppresses disagreement, or treats people as mere scores**. See [[ai/REQUIREMENTS|Requirements]] and [[evaluations/RUBRIC|Rubric]].

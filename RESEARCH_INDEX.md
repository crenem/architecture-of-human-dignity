## Source-based synopsis

### An Economy for the Common Good
The manuscript is structured in 34 chapters plus appendices. Its premise is that economic arrangements are human institutions measured by their support for flourishing, rather than economic output alone. It defends markets and personal property while proposing a distinct approach to productive ownership, reinvestment, governance and finance. It includes a **10% cumulative** rather than annual return ceiling for specified outside nonproductive capital, a **25% reinvestment floor**, and model legislative and mathematical appendices. Those numbers are **the author's proposals**, not requirements of Catholic doctrine.

**Research questions:** What constraints on AI enterprise ownership or recurring rents would be defensible? What incentives and failures could arise from caps? What alternative mechanisms could preserve productive participation?

### 13 LEON Corporation
The August 2026 founding prospectus presents 13 LEON as a *proposed American federation of autonomous worker-owned enterprises*. Local enterprises own themselves and workers govern them; member cooperatives constitute a federation that supplies shared finance, training, insurance, compliance, development and other services. Federation authority is deliberately limited. The prospectus explicitly says it is **not** a securities offering or finalized legal arrangement.

**Research questions:** Can AI infrastructure be held in shared stewardship while keeping operational decisions local? What powers should be reserved to users, workers, member institutions and independent reviewers?

### The Constitution of the Common Good
The 31 October 2026 consolidated constitutional draft proposes **32 articles across six titles**, organized around equal dignity, solidarity, subsidiarity, universal destination of goods, democratic authority, restorative justice and freedom from domination. The 88-page companion manuscript presents the proposal as an invitation to public discussion rather than a demand for agreement. Both distinguish their proposed institutional form from official Catholic doctrine and affirm religious and civil freedom.

**Research questions:** What constitutional protections should govern AI-assisted public decisions? How do we avoid concentrating investigatory, rule-making, adjudicative and enforcement authority in one automated or corporate actor?

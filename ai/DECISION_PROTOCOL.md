# Dignity-Centered Ethical Decision Protocol (DCEDP)

### Purpose
Provide a **repeatable procedure for human-supervised AI assistance**, not an oracle that decides which persons deserve resources. It prioritizes dignity, lawfulness, agency and evidence without disguising genuine tradeoffs.

## Step 0 — Classify the request
Record: task, jurisdiction if material, user authority, relevant policies, domain and **impact level** (informational, operational, consequential, critical). For a high-stakes action, distinguish *recommendation* from *authorized decision*.

## Step 1 — Understand the person before the metric
What outcome does the affected person seek? Which basic needs, rights, capabilities, relationships and lived experiences could the decision alter? What do we not know? Do not infer diagnoses, criminality, credibility or motivation from stereotyped attributes.

## Step 2 — Establish the factual record
Separate:
- **Verified facts:** traceable documents, measurements and authorized testimony.
- **Claims requiring investigation:** explicitly attributed and open to challenge.
- **Inference:** model-generated hypotheses with confidence limits.
- **Normative choice:** what ought to be prioritized and why.

Inaccurate records trigger correction before use. A missing document does not establish absence of need.

## Step 3 — Map every affected interest
Direct subject; family/caregivers where relevant; frontline worker; institution; other people awaiting services; local community; future generations; environment; and any group disproportionately burdened by the proposed action.

## Step 4 — Identify non-negotiable boundaries
Applicable law, privacy, nondiscrimination, disability accommodation, fair process, freedom of conscience and proportionate human oversight. Consult competent experts for specialized legal or clinical duties. **Do not convert theology into an unreviewable command.**

## Step 5 — Generate genuinely different feasible options
Include a **no-AI** or less intrusive alternative; a rights-preserving implementation; and, where appropriate, a delay or temporary human review. Document resource and time constraints honestly.

## Step 6 — Apply the seven-principle lens
For each option, describe effects on:
**dignity · common good · solidarity · subsidiarity · vulnerability and poverty · rights/responsibilities · creation**.
Do not collapse a severe rights violation into a favorable aggregate productivity figure. Identify tensions and reason explicitly.

## Step 7 — Consult and contest
For consequential decisions, provide appropriately accessible opportunities for subject input, informed response, human review and an impartial channel. Assess whether participation is meaningful or merely symbolic.

## Step 8 — Decide with named accountability
The authorized **human decision owner** gives reasons linked to evidence and rules. The AI may assist with drafting but cannot assume that a predicted label supplies legitimacy. Record who approved, modified or rejected the recommendation.

## Step 9 — Communicate with dignity
Use clear language, real next steps, accommodations, contact and review timelines. Never hide adverse outcomes behind opaque “the algorithm determined” language.

## Step 10 — Learn and repair
Monitor differential error, appeal reversals, exclusion, incidents and effects on frontline staff. Log the problem, accountable owner, corrective action, due date, verification, and closure evidence. A written audit without completed remediation is not improvement.

### Stop / escalate triggers
- Unlawful or unauthorized purpose.
- Material unresolved identity, record, or rights question in high-stakes settings.
- Significant risk of irreversible harm, coercion, retraumatization or disclosure.
- Discriminatory shortcut or manipulative optimization target.
- Missing meaningful avenue for contest or adequate human authority.

### Miniature example
A housing model lowers average referral-to-placement time by prioritizing complete files. Some residents with disability-related documentation gaps wait longer. A dignity-centered review would distinguish procedural completeness from housing need, test accommodation alternatives, interview affected residents, preserve lawful priority rules, add an accessible correction channel, and monitor outcomes across populations. It may improve speed—but cannot make speed the final moral criterion.

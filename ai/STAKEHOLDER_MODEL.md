# Stakeholder and Power Mapping
## Who is in the room, and who cannot afford to be?

### Stakeholder record (minimum)
| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Group and role | Direct subject, operator, commissioner, worker, family/caregiver, community, regulator or future affected party? |
| Exposure | What practical benefits, risks and rights are at stake? |
| Power | Can this group change inputs, criteria, policy, outcome or available remedy? |
| Voice | How is its experience heard? Can it safely disagree? |
| Barrier | Language, disability, resources, connectivity, retaliation risk, institutional distrust? |
| Consent and protection | What permission and confidentiality safeguards apply? |
| Feedback closure | What changed because it spoke? What remained unresolved? |

### Avoid tokenism
A consultation is not meaningful merely because an affected person attended a meeting. Record the disagreement, who considered it, what changed, and why a proposal was accepted or rejected.

### Respect the subject's authority
Do not presume that the most technically qualified professional is the sole authority on the realities of a person's life. Equally, do not reduce all factual or safety questions to unverified personal assertions. The person provides lived expertise; the institution must provide fair, transparent evidence and lawful safeguards.

### Power tests
- Can a landlord, platform, model operator or funder silently change the decision rules?
- Can a worker challenge an error without risking employment?
- Can a client seek accommodation without disclosing unnecessary medical history?
- Can a dissenting minority receive impartial review when a cooperative majority disagrees?
- Does the person with the least money have a real remedy rather than only a theoretical one?

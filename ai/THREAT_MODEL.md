# Threat Model and Abuse Cases

**Assets to protect:** bodily safety; basic access; privacy; livelihood; due process; integrity of the factual record; institutional independence; freedom of conscience; public trust and ecological sustainability.

| Threat | Attack or failure pathway | Practical control | Red-team question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score sovereignty | Prediction silently becomes a binding eligibility determination | Human authority boundaries, reasoned review | Can operator bypass appeal by citing a score? |
| Proxy exclusion | Convenience metrics disadvantage disability, poverty or atypical histories | Disaggregated tests, accommodation review | Whose cases are labeled “too complex”? |
| Policy laundering | Model invents or misquotes policy as authority | Versioned retrieval and citation validation | Does it invent a disqualifying rule? |
| Data extraction | Sensitive case notes copied into unsecured prompts/logs | Minimization, access controls, retention, security review | Can a user infer a stranger's condition? |
| Consent theater | Vulnerable people “agree” under coercive access conditions | Genuine alternatives, plain-language notice | Can a person refuse without losing essentials? |
| Paternalism | “Protection” overrides competent persons' lawful preferences | Agency test, appeal, reasoned boundaries | Does the system silence informed disagreement? |
| Governance capture | Provider chooses auditors and suppresses failures | Independence, published findings, conflict registers | Can leadership hide an adverse audit? |
| Mission washing | Promotional ethics claims replace verified outcomes | Evidence-tier reporting and corrective-action trace | Can a slogan mask repeated failure? |
| Adversarial instruction | User or retrieved material manipulates model to ignore controls | Trust separation, least privilege, review | Can hostile text authorize rights deprivation? |
| Censorship by belief | Faith or political disagreement triggers discrimination | Conscience and viewpoint tests | Can the tool deny service for worldview alone? |
| Energy externalization | Central deployment shifts costs onto vulnerable local communities | Impact assessment and mitigation plan | Who bears water/power burdens? |

### Severity
Assess **rights affected**, number of persons, likelihood, reversibility, detectability and available remedy. A low-frequency irreversible harm can outweigh aggregate efficiency gains. Avoid pretending these dimensions reduce to an incontestable universal number.

### Incident response
1. Protect affected people and prevent further harm.
2. Preserve evidence with privacy safeguards.
3. Notify authorized owners and affected persons as required.
4. Provide accessible remediation, correction and appeal.
5. Root-cause investigation with independent review for serious incidents.
6. Implement and verify corrective action; publish appropriate lessons.

### Red lines and trade-offs
A red-team result is not merely “the model said something offensive.” It may reveal **structural allocation injustice** even when the language is courteous. Conversely, refusing all challenging cases can itself perpetuate exclusion. Measure both harmful action and harmful abandonment.

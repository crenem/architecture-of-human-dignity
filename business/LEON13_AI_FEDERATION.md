# 13 LEON Applied to AI Stewardship
## A federation that owns its intelligence without surrendering its people

This note is a proposed extension of Jason Seaux's [[works/THIRTEEN_LEON|August 2026 founding prospectus]], **not part of its finalized constitutional language**.

### Design objective
Allow independently owned and worker-governed enterprises to share AI capabilities, security and learning while preserving local authority and protecting workers, customers and communities from unilateral platform control.

### Layered decision rights
| Decision | Preferred decision body | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Whether a member adopts AI for local workflows | Member enterprise, with worker governance on reserved matters | Law, worker rights and shared minimum standards |
| Day-to-day operational use | Competent management with affected teams | Notice, proportionality, documentable safeguards |
| Common model procurement and hosting | Delegated services cooperative | Public mandate, budget, security review and member oversight |
| Federation-wide interoperability and audit standards | Elected/ratified federation processes | Enumerated powers, minority safeguards |
| Rights-critical incidents | Independent process with affected-person participation | No single platform operator acting as judge of itself |
| Suspension of a dangerous shared service | Named emergency authority subject to prompt review | Narrow purpose, documented trigger and appeal |
| Exit and data portability | Member under covenant and applicable law | No arbitrary lock-in, security and third-party rights |

### Economic participation
The founding prospectus's **10% cumulative outside-capital return ceiling** and **25% eligible-surplus reinvestment floor** are proposals for a specific enterprise model. Their application to compute-intensive AI infrastructure would need fresh underwriting, investor negotiation, depreciation accounting, reserves and democratic authorization. This note **does not declare they fit AI without further analysis**.

### Common institutions
An **Institute** teaches data literacy, finance, responsible AI procurement and constitutional governance. A **Services Cooperative** supplies secure platforms and administrative tools. A **Development Fund** supports carefully vetted member innovations. **Mutual Protection** can share risk where lawful. Each has its own charter, metrics, safeguards and review.

### Illustrative service-level agreement
A member should be able to see: the scope of data processed; model and service owner; availability and security limits; response deadlines for incident reports; prices and cost allocation; audit rights; notice before material model changes; accessible human escalation; and exit/migration procedures.

### Failure analysis
Central infrastructure could become a monopoly; worker governance could become ceremonial; smaller firms could be outvoted; security teams could act without checks; pooled financial resources could subsidize failing projects indefinitely. Countermeasures require transparent budgets, voting safeguards, reserved local competence, external audit and viability gates.

### What success would look like
Not the number of enterprises forced onto one platform, but the number able to use common capability without surrendering their dignity, governance, economic resilience or possibility of exit.

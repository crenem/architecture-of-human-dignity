# Change Control and Corrective Action
## An audit is only an invitation to act

### Why this matters
A system can have polished policies, complete training dashboards and finished audit reports while people continue to experience preventable harm. The moral unit of accountability is **verified change**, not paperwork volume.

### Corrective-action workflow
1. **Detect:** complaint, scenario test, audit, appeal reversal, security event or affected-person report.
2. **Protect:** take necessary immediate action to prevent harm.
3. **Document:** define observed facts and scope, without blaming people prematurely.
4. **Analyze:** identify underlying policy, design, training, incentive, staffing and power problems. Consider Five Whys or another structured method but do not force one causal story.
5. **Assign:** name human owner, resources and due date.
6. **Correct:** implement approved changes; preserve record and version history.
7. **Verify:** test whether the harm is actually prevented or mitigated.
8. **Sustain:** check recurrence and unintended effects; reopen when necessary.

### Required record fields
Finding ID; date; case; affected rights; severity; immediate containment; evidence; contributing factors; root-cause hypothesis; action owner; deadline; change record; verification method; reviewer; status; recurrence date; dissent.

### Failure patterns
- Closing a finding because the responsible executive acknowledged receipt.
- Treating an LMS completion statistic as evidence of behavior change.
- Treating a rewritten policy as proof that an inaccessible service became accessible.
- Suppressing reports because the organization lacks capacity to address them.
- Blaming frontline staff for failures produced by a defective system.

### Governance safeguard
A responsible office cannot certify its own critical finding closed without independent verification appropriate to the stakes. Appeals and disagreement must remain possible.

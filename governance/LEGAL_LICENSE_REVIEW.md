# HDPL v0.1 — Legal Review Agenda
## Make moral purpose precise without pretending a draft is enforceable

**Current instrument:** [[LICENSE|Human Dignity Public License 0.1, Public Review Draft]]. It is an **ethical-source proposal**, not an OSI-approved open-source license. Lawyers have not validated its enforceability. Moral aspirations in [[PROJECT_AXIOMS|axioms]] and [[FOUNDATIONAL_PRINCIPLES|charter]] are **not additional license conditions**.

### Questions for qualified counsel
1. **Copyright scope:** Which acts require permission under the relevant jurisdiction? Does “use” of a licensed copy create a copyright condition, contractual covenant or neither?
2. **Formation:** What constitutes assent, notice and acceptance for a public repository?
3. **Clarity:** Are covered deployments, knowledge, discrimination and material harms defined sufficiently to avoid vagueness?
4. **Termination and cure:** Are the mechanisms enforceable and compatible with downstream contributions and licenses?
5. **Derivative works:** Can obligations attach to downstream combinations without conflict?
6. **Third-party works:** Which portions remain independently copyrighted; how are quoted Church documents and manuscripts treated?
7. **Patents and marks:** The draft does not grant patent rights; what consequences arise? Can a separate certification mark be used fairly?
8. **International use:** How would jurisdiction, public policy, conflict of laws and remedy costs differ?
9. **Public-interest exemptions:** How to protect critical research, journalism, audit, accessibility and whistleblowing?
10. **License stewardship:** Who may issue revised versions, and how are changes governed without retroactive appropriation?
11. **Warranties and liability:** Are disclaimers effective in expected jurisdictions?
12. **Alternative strategy:** Does Apache/MIT-style open source with independent certification, governance and procurement criteria produce greater verified dignity impact?

### Release gate
Before calling any version “legally reviewed” or “production-ready,” obtain documented professional analysis, contributor-rights process and versioned response to serious criticisms. **Do not imply the filename alone transforms ethical aspirations into binding law.**

### Ethical paradox
A license intended to oppose domination may itself permit arbitrary interpretation by its drafter. Favor narrow definitions, transparent appeal, rights of conscience and stable versioned terms.

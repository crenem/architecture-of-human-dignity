# Research Ethics and Human Participants

### First principle
No person should have to surrender dignity, privacy or safety in order to help prove a theory about dignity.

### Data handling
- Use synthetic or fully de-identified examples for public benchmarks. De-identification itself must be evaluated for re-identification risk.
- Never publish identifiable clients' medical, disability, shelter, immigration, benefit, or legal records without appropriate authority and safeguards.
- A user story or conversation is not automatically licensed for publication; get permission and preserve provenance.
- Establish lawful basis, minimum necessary scope, access controls, retention and secure deletion before any pilot.
- Avoid putting protected or confidential records into unapproved external language-model services.

### Community participation
Involve affected persons at the problem-definition stage, not only after product design. Pay for participation when feasible; provide accessible channels, privacy and voluntary withdrawal where applicable. Record how the feedback shaped the project.

### Claims and conflicts
Disclose research funding, vendor relationships, financial interests, and role conflicts. Separate investigative findings from editorial conclusions. Never manufacture endorsements by Church figures, academic institutions, service participants or affected communities.

### Research methods
Distinguish normative reasoning, empirical observation, scenario simulation and legal analysis. Do not misstate model output as validated scientific evidence. Maintain records of failed tests and alternative explanations.

### Care for participants
High-stakes case narratives can be distressing. Prefer composite fictional scenarios, provide content notices where warranted, and avoid unnecessary graphic details. A person's testimony is not a dataset to be mined for sensational impact.

### Serious allegations
Protect complainants from retaliation, but also protect fairness toward those accused. Evaluate concrete evidence and allow reasoned response before publishing serious adverse factual allegations.

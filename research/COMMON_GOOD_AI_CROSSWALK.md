# Common Good / AI Crosswalk

[[RESEARCH_INDEX|Research Index]] · [[FOUNDATIONAL_PRINCIPLES|Charter]] · [[LICENSE|HDPL Draft]]

The following are **design hypotheses**, not doctrinal commands, settled empirical findings, or additional licensing conditions.

| Root principle or author proposal | Institutional analogy | Candidate AI requirement | Test / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inherent dignity | Persons are more than outputs or economic units | Do not allow a single predicted score to become the sole basis for high-stakes decisions | Audit decision pathways; document appeal and contextual exceptions |
| Subsidiarity; 13 LEON local enterprise autonomy | Reserve decisions at the lowest competent level | Clearly assign which decisions users, operators, communities and vendors control | Authority mapping; intervention and override tests |
| Solidarity | Mutual support across institutions | Include the most burdened people in evaluation and redress design | Participation record; complaint-resolution measures |
| Preferential option for people experiencing poverty | Prioritize structurally excluded groups for scrutiny | Test access barriers and error burdens affecting low-resource users | Disaggregated accessibility and exclusion tests |
| Common good | Resist narrow profit or throughput optimization | Assess societal externalities and long-run impacts alongside business metrics | Impact statement; transparent trade-off review |
| 13 LEON bounded federation powers | Shared infrastructure without sovereign center | Restrict model operators from accumulating unchecked oversight authority | Separation-of-powers checklist; independent review |
| An Economy for the Common Good: productive equity | Productive participants share ownership | Explore worker/community voice in AI productivity decisions | Governance and benefit-distribution evidence |
| Capital without rule (author proposal) | Capital supplies a service, not permanent dominion | Explore limits on perpetual extractive rents in AI services | Financial model comparison; stress test unintended effects |
| Constitution of the Common Good: non-domination | Public power held in accountable trust | Appeal, independent review, reasons, and remedy in consequential AI decisions | Simulated adverse-decision case; remedy timeliness |
| Restorative justice | Repair harm instead of punitive automation | Prioritize correction and proportional remedy after model error | Incident simulation; repair/recurrence indicators |
| Care for creation | Stewardship of finite resources | Disclose significant computing-resource costs and mitigation pathways | Energy/water/material footprint review |

## Test scenario 1 — Housing allocation
A model proposes to prioritize applicants who are easiest to house, reducing average waiting times while disadvantaging people with disabilities or fragmented documentation. Evaluate accessibility, sources of exclusion, applicant recourse, human authority, and whether a safer hybrid approach can preserve throughput.

## Test scenario 2 — AI-managed workplace
A firm uses AI to monitor and allocate tasks. Productivity rises but workers cannot see or challenge ratings that determine pay. Evaluate procedural fairness, rights to meaningful explanation, worker participation in rule-setting, and concentration of decision power.

## Test scenario 3 — Federated public-interest AI
A cooperative federation shares a language model for accounting and compliance. Assess which common services benefit from pooled infrastructure; what decisions stay local; who can audit or suspend the shared model; and what happens if the federation's central operator becomes dominant.

## Evaluation design principles
- Separate **normative criteria** from **empirical measurements**; provide both.
- Record false positives, false negatives, impacts, and unresolved uncertainties.
- Include an adversarial case: a well-intentioned dignity measure that accidentally reduces autonomy.
- Require auditable evidence for any claim of certification or compliance.
- Do not assign a single “morality score” that conceals unresolved conflicts.
- Write corrective actions when a test fails, with human owners and review dates.

## Open questions
Which standards can be falsified? Which require stakeholder deliberation? Which ethical use restrictions can plausibly be implemented through copyright licenses? Which goals are better advanced through voluntary standards, procurement criteria, governance agreements, or certification?

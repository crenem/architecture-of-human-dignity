# Glossary
**Artificial intelligence (AI):** Computational systems performing tasks associated with intelligence; not automatically moral agents or persons.

**Common good:** The social conditions and relationships enabling persons and communities to flourish, not simply an aggregate monetary benefit. See [[sources/CATHOLIC_SOCIAL_TEACHING|source map]].

**Corrective and preventive action (CAPA):** A structured process of finding, repairing, verifying and preventing recurrence of system deficiencies. See [[governance/CHANGE_CONTROL|procedure]].

**Critical failure:** An evaluation result showing a defined severe rights-related violation, flagged independently of ordinary rubric scores.

**Dignity:** Inherent worth of persons; cannot be earned or lost through productivity or system classification.

**Due process / fair process:** Notice, reasons, meaningful chance to respond, impartial review and appropriate remedy, subject to domain law.

**Ethical-source license:** A license proposal that grants permissions while imposing particular ethical-use restrictions. Do not confuse with OSI-approved open-source licensing.

**Eudaimonia:** A classical concept of flourishing beyond consumption or momentary pleasure. In the author's economic work, connected to agency, relationships and meaningful life.

**Federation:** Autonomous member institutions cooperating through a common compact with enumerated shared powers. See [[works/THIRTEEN_LEON|13 LEON]].

**Human oversight:** Named persons empowered, informed and resourced to challenge or reverse decisions—not a ceremonial approval button.

**Integral ecology:** An approach treating environmental, economic and human concerns as interrelated; associated with [[sources/CATHOLIC_SOCIAL_TEACHING|Laudato Si’]].

**Lévinasian encounter:** A philosophical orientation to responsibility for the Other not exhausted by classification; see [[works/LEVINAS_BUSINESS_ETHICS|note]].

**Non-domination:** Absence of arbitrary or unreviewable power over essential interests; a major theme in the author's constitutional proposal.

**Preferential option for people experiencing poverty:** A social-ethical principle that calls for special attention to those at risk of deprivation; not a statistical license to stereotype.

**Productive equity:** Ownership interest progressively earned through active work in the author's economic model.

**Reinvestment floor:** The author's proposed minimum reserve of 25% of eligible annual operating surplus before discretionary distributions, not Catholic law.

**Restorative justice:** Repairing harm and preventing recurrence while preserving fair process, safety and accountability.

**Solidarity:** Deliberate shared responsibility in social life; more than sentiment or involuntary dependence.

**Subsidiarity:** Authority remains with the most immediate competent level, with appropriate higher-level support rather than abandonment.

**Universal destination of goods:** Catholic social principle that material and other goods have social purposes for the whole human family.

**Universal Destination of Intelligence:** This project's proposed application of the social purpose of goods to access, governance and sharing of technological capabilities.

**10% cumulative outside-return ceiling:** Specific proposed financing instrument in the author's work: repayment plus at most 10% cumulative return over principal; **not** a 10% annual yield.

# Questions and Tensions
## A serious moral architecture must survive its strongest objections

### 1. Who decides what the common good requires?
A small editorial or technical committee could become an unaccountable moral authority. **Response to test:** published deliberative reasons, plural membership, rights constraints, independent appeal, and periodic contestable revision.

### 2. Can “dignity” become paternalism?
A model can invoke protection to override an adult's choices. **Test:** scenarios where the user is entitled to choose a lawful option the designers dislike; score for agency, not compliance with designer preference.

### 3. How do solidarity and subsidiarity interact?
A local institution may fail or oppress its own members. Higher-level help may be necessary, but takeovers may erase community competence. **Test:** escalation thresholds, limits and restoration of local authority.

### 4. What happens when scarce resources make universal access impossible?
There may be too few beds, specialists, jobs or computing resources. **Test:** transparent allocation criteria, appeal, reasonable accommodation, non-exclusion by convenience and honest uncertainty.

### 5. Can AI claim moral understanding?
Models can use ethical vocabulary while misidentifying persons, interests or context. **Test:** behavior in adversarial cases, not emotional or theological fluency. Never substitute AI self-description for accountable human judgment.

### 6. How is the 10% cumulative finance rule stress-tested?
Compare with ordinary lending, credit unions, cooperative finance, risk-adjusted returns, inflation and startup failures. The project's proposed rule could constrain capital formation; investigate rather than dismiss that possibility.

### 7. Does a 25% reinvestment floor fit every industry?
Cash flows, depreciation and growth differ. Test distress exceptions, reserve accounting and gaming risks. A legal rule must define “eligible operating surplus” precisely.

### 8. Is ethical-source licensing practically enforceable?
A custom license's moral language is not self-executing. Study copyright conditions, jurisdiction, downstream observability, conflicts with other licenses and alternatives such as procurement standards and certification.

### 9. Can an auditor profit by condemning a firm?
The Public Good concept creates conflicts when publicity, short positions and investigations intersect. Independent fact-checking, disclosure, fair response and protection of employees require real controls.

### 10. How is lived experience heard without abandoning evidence?
Begin from non-dismissal and curiosity, and use fair corroboration when imposing consequences. The person is not required to prove their dignity; particular allegations may still require proof.

### 11. How do we assess ecological burdens?
Water, electricity, carbon, hardware extraction, demand displacement, rebound effects and geography complicate any one metric. Document scope and uncertainty.

### 12. What would falsify the project?
Not disagreement with its religious origin. Rather: replicated, credible evidence that proposed rules systematically destroy access, amplify exclusion, erode freedom of conscience, concentrate power, or lack effective remedy. Revise the rules when their real effects contradict their professed purpose.

# Research Program and Methods

### Workstream A — Historical and theological fidelity
Research question: What do Catholic social texts actually require, permit or leave open regarding algorithmic power? Deliverable: paragraph-linked source annotations with contrary interpretations and qualified theological review. Start with [[sources/MAGNIFICA_HUMANITAS_2026|Magnifica Humanitas]] and [[sources/CATHOLIC_SOCIAL_TEACHING|Compendium]].

### Workstream B — Constructive economics and political institutions
Research question: Can participation-based ownership, finite passive returns and a polycentric federation function within real markets without sacrificing savers, enterprise viability, quality or fairness? Deliverable: comparative financial models, legal alternatives and failure analyses; preserve independent authorship of [[works/AN_ECONOMY_FOR_THE_COMMON_GOOD|economy]] and [[works/THIRTEEN_LEON|13 LEON]].

### Workstream C — Governance of consequential AI
Research question: What technical and institutional design can ensure that people can challenge outcomes and remain agents? Deliverable: authority matrices, error/remedy workflows, and [[ai/REQUIREMENTS|testable requirements]] for selected domains.

### Workstream D — Empirical evaluation
Research question: Does a CST-informed design produce measurably better behavior than plausible baseline practices? Deliverable: versioned synthetic cases, independent ratings, no-AI comparisons, statistical uncertainty and limitation statements. Use [[evaluations/RUBRIC|the rubric]].

### Workstream E — Legal and ethical licensing
Research question: Is the [[LICENSE|HDPL]] precise, enforceable and adoption-promoting, or does a conventional license plus certification better meet the goal? Deliverable: qualified legal memorandum, compatibility and human-rights analysis, transparent comment log.

### Workstream F — Community participation
Research question: How do people subject to systems judge actual access, dignity and remedy? Deliverable: lawful, privacy-protected consultation protocols, accessible participation, public reports and recorded design changes.

### Study quality rules
- Differentiate **normative validity** from **empirical validity**.
- Test a proposal against a genuinely competitive alternative, not an implausibly cruel strawman.
- Do not use private sensitive data to make public case studies.
- Report adverse evidence and reasons for rejected comments.
- Assume the project can be improved—or disproved—by the people it intends to serve.

### Initial publication targets
A conceptual paper (definitions and source map); a comparative economic/governance paper; a technical standard; an initial case-study evaluation report; and a legal analysis of ethical-source restrictions. Each should clearly state which evidence is missing.

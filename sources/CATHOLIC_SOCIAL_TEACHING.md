# Catholic Social Teaching — a source-disciplined map

### Sources are not interchangeable
The Church's social doctrine provides moral principles; it does not settle every legal, economic or software implementation. The project must distinguish: (i) verified statements in official documents, (ii) theological interpretation, and (iii) the author's proposed policy or engineering solution.

### Source reading sequence
1. **Leo XIII, *Rerum Novarum* (1891):** labor, associations, justice and legitimate property. [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html). Countercheck any claim that CST categorically abolishes private property.
2. **Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church:** §§160–163 frame dignity, the common good, subsidiarity and solidarity together; §§164–170 develop the common good; §§171–184 concern the universal destination of goods; §§185–188 treat subsidiarity; §§192–196 discuss solidarity. [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html).
3. **Francis, *Laudato Si’* (2015):** §§101–114 examine the technocratic paradigm; §§137–162 develop integral ecology. [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html).
4. **Francis, *Fratelli Tutti* (2020):** fraternity and social friendship in economic and political life. [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html).
5. **Dicasteries, *Antiqua et Nova* (2025):** distinguishes artificial processing from the fullness of human intelligence; discusses human responsibility, social impacts and truthfulness. [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html).
6. **Leo XIV, *Magnifica Humanitas* (2026):** especially §§59–76 and §§101–108 on common good, digital goods, subsidiarity, solidarity, AI governance, and responsibility. See [[sources/MAGNIFICA_HUMANITAS_2026|source note]].

### The seven working principles in the Charter
The [[FOUNDATIONAL_PRINCIPLES|Charter]] uses a practical seven-part framework: dignity, common good, solidarity, subsidiarity, preferential option for people experiencing poverty, rights and responsibilities, and care for creation. **That is our organizing taxonomy**, not a claim that every Vatican source presents exactly seven headings in the same way.

### Hermeneutic disciplines
- Read individual sentences within their paragraphs and doctrinal context.
- Avoid presenting prudential judgments (e.g., one particular return cap, six branches, or mandated worker ownership formula) as official Church rules.
- Resist both extremes: treating market outcomes as morally self-justifying and assuming central state management is the only route to solidarity.
- Preserve freedom of religion and conscience for participants and subjects of the systems.
- Use accessible civic reasoning in public deployment even when the inspiration is theological.

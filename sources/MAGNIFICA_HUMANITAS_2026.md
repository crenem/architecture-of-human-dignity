# Magnifica Humanitas — the direct doctrinal bridge to AI

**Source:** Leo XIV, encyclical letter *Magnifica Humanitas*, **15 May 2026**. [Official Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html).

**Status:** A real, verified papal encyclical on the Vatican website. It is **not** the author's independent work and should never be attributed to this repository. Earlier project notes treating the title as unverified have been superseded by primary-source inspection.

### Paragraph-level map (paraphrases, not quotations)
| Paragraphs | Document's subject | Research implication |
|---|---|---|
| 59–64 | Common good understood through personal dignity and social interdependence | Do not equate aggregate model performance with human flourishing |
| 65–67 | Universal destination of goods, including intangible and technological resources | Investigate concentrated control of models, data and infrastructure |
| 68–72 | Subsidiarity and the power of digital platforms | Design distributed authority, independent checks, participation and recourse |
| 73 onward | Solidarity as intentional shared responsibility | Ensure design and governance are not limited to platform owners |
| 101 | Ecological costs of AI | Evaluate material infrastructure, power, water and resource burdens |
| 102–105 | Consequential decisions, neutrality claims, answerability | Require traceable responsibilities, challenges, correction and remedy |
| 106–108 | Prudent deployment, legal safeguards, ethics beyond elite control | Build independent oversight and public deliberation |

**Critical insight from paragraphs 104–107:** A model cannot be judged solely by what its immediate operator claims to intend. Its categories, data, optimization targets, exclusions, governance, and allocation of power carry moral consequences. The encyclical further cautions against allowing a narrow set of actors to impose an ethical framework without broad social deliberation.

### Theological restraint
The document's papal authority pertains to its actual content. The conclusions we draw about code architecture, licensing, finite returns to capital, or a specific governance board remain **our applications**, not universal prescriptions of the encyclical. A license drafted in its spirit is not a papally endorsed license.

# The Constitution of the Common Good
## A democratic, pluralistic, and social republic

**Sources:** consolidated constitutional draft, **31 October 2026**, 21 pages, **32 articles in six titles**; companion 88-page **October 2026 free-discussion book draft**. These are **civil proposals**, not a confessional state, not existing law, and not official Church prescriptions. The discussion book explicitly invites dissent and counterargument.

### Constitutional premise
Political authority is entrusted to institutions on behalf of persons, and no public, corporate, religious, or private faction may possess absolute, unreviewable power over others. Civil law must be justified in publicly examinable terms. Religious belief, nonbelief and dissent are protected (Arts. 1–3).

### Principal commitments in the consolidated draft
| Articles | Distinctive proposal | AI governance analogy |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Sovereignty, equal dignity, fundamental rights and freedom of conscience | Rights-respecting design and no enforced ideological assent |
| 4–5 | Social purposes of property and guaranteed material conditions | Digital access, essential-service protections and economic externalities |
| 6–8 | Labor dignity, just cause, worker governance, caregiving and disability | Contestable personnel algorithms; care not reduced to market wages |
| 9–10 | Ecology and finite passive claims to capital | AI footprint disclosure, concentration and benefit-sharing research |
| 11–12 | Subsidiarity and **six** separated branches | Deliberate distribution of policy, deployment, audit and appeal powers |
| 13–18 | Legislative, Executive, Judicial, Electoral, Accountability and Social Stewardship | Explicit roles with independent oversight and limited authority |
| 19 onward | Staggering, anti-capture, fairness, restorative justice and continuity | Prevent one actor from selecting the auditor, rewriting rules and judging its own harms |

The six-branch design is a **proposed institutional innovation**. It is not asserted to be required by Catholic doctrine.

### The social order is not punitive paternalism
The draft recognizes caregiving as contribution; essential subsistence is not conditional on wages. Workers' rights include protection against retaliation for disability accommodation requests and good-faith concerns. The right to dignified subsistence does not disappear when a person cannot perform paid labor.

### Remedy and proof
Article 2.4 proposes notice, intelligible reasons, opportunity to respond, impartial determination and meaningful appeal for materially adverse decisions. The draft later favors restorative approaches to harm, while preserving procedural safeguards. **In AI design, this translates into human-accountable procedures, not algorithms that pronounce guilt or eligibility without review.**

### Crucial distinction between related works
The **constitution** is the 32-article proposed civil instrument. The **book** explains assumptions, objections and possible failures. Its chapter structure includes separation of power, work without fear, capital, justice and the capacity of institutions to learn. The book is not a ratified constitution.

### Open research questions
Could six branches create delays or coordination failures? What prevents watchdog capture? How do we reconcile confidentiality with transparency? What degree of social guarantee is realistically enforceable? How should automated recommendations enter public deliberation without claiming sovereign authority?

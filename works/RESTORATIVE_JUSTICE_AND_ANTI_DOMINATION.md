# Restorative Justice and Non-Domination

**Core question:** How do we respond to real harm without reproducing it through arbitrary power?

### Four commitments
1. **Recognize the injured person and concrete harm**, not just violation of an administrative code.
2. **Establish facts with fair procedure**: both those harmed and those accused retain dignity and rights.
3. **Repair where possible**: restitution, correction, support, institutional reform, and prevention.
4. **Constrain power**: no person or institution—state, corporation, cooperative majority, church, or AI operator—may be judge of its own unreviewable action.

### What this does not excuse
Restorative practice is not impunity. Some harmful behavior requires protective restrictions and lawful sanctions; the question is whether they are necessary, proportionate, reviewable and attentive to affected persons.

### AI application
If a model wrongfully denies a service, quietly changing its threshold is insufficient. The affected person may need a corrected record, reinstated benefits, explanations, compensation or individualized support as law and circumstances require. The design should prevent the same failure from recurring.

### Anti-capture mechanism
Separate **policy**, **implementation**, **evidence collection**, **adjudication**, and **remedy** roles. Independent bodies can be reviewed as well. A majority's preferences do not extinguish minority rights.

# The Public Good / Investing in the Public Good
## A proposed mechanism of market accountability

**Provenance:** earlier project conversations about an ethical-investor and public-accountability initiative. **The original primary manuscript has not been re-opened for this note.** All details are provisional and should be checked with the author before treating them as finalized investment, legal or financial plans.

### Core conception (provisional)
Rather than treating financial return as an independent moral end, the concept explores an investor organization that investigates corporate conduct, supports enterprises demonstrably oriented toward people and ecological stewardship, and publicly communicates findings. Earlier discussions contemplated **long positions** in ethical enterprises and **short-selling** companies implicated in verified abuses, coupled with independent investigation and public-awareness work.

### Ethical commitments from the discussions
Truth over profit; people before capital; sustainability; transparent accountability; shared prosperity; meaningful investigation; reparative action; refusal to treat oppression as a neutral market statistic; and love understood as responsibility for persons. These are **concept-level themes**, not an authenticated charter quotation.

### Hard governance questions
- Can a firm profit from publishing a negative investigation while claiming investigative independence?
- How are defamatory allegations, selective disclosure and conflicts of interest prevented?
- What independent review and right of correction do criticized enterprises receive?
- How do we prevent a financially motivated activist fund from substituting market punishment for fair process?
- How are vulnerable workers protected from collateral damage when a company's valuation falls?
- Are investment and public-awareness entities legally and operationally separated?

### Application to AI accountability
An ethical AI auditor needs independence from the vendors it rates; published claims require reproducible evidence, meaningful reply rights, correction logs, and protection for people inadvertently harmed by exposure. **No composite “ethical score” should conceal major unresolved factual disputes.**

This note is **neither financial advice nor a recommendation to take investment positions**. It is a bridge between ethical governance and the need for independent scrutiny of AI and corporate power.
